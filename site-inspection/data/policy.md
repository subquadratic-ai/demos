Harbor Line Builders — Site Safety Rules (fictional, written for this demo)

Section 1. Personal protective equipment
1.1 Hard hats. Everyone on an active site wears a hard hat where there is any overhead work or falling-object hazard.
1.2 High-visibility vests. Workers near moving equipment or traffic wear a high-visibility vest.
1.3 Eye protection. Safety glasses are worn while cutting, grinding, drilling or chipping.
1.4 Footwear. Safety-toe boots are worn on every active site.

Section 2. Fall protection
2.1 Six-foot rule. Any worker on a surface with an unprotected side or edge six feet or more above a lower level is protected by a guardrail, a safety net, or a personal fall arrest system (harness and lanyard tied off to a rated anchor).
2.2 Roofs. Work on a roof edge, ridge or steep slope requires a personal fall arrest system or guardrails. Standing or walking on roof framing without fall protection is prohibited.
2.3 Anchors. Lanyards are tied off to a rated anchor point, never to conduit, guardrail posts or loose material.
2.4 Holes. Floor and roof openings are covered and marked or guarded.

Section 3. Ladders
3.1 Setup. Ladders stand on firm, level footing and extend at least three feet above the landing they reach.
3.2 Use. Face the ladder and keep three points of contact. Never stand on the top step or top cap.
3.3 Roof access. Ladders used for roof access during residential framing are paired with fall protection at the landing.

Section 4. Scaffolds
4.1 Scaffold guardrails. Scaffolds over ten feet have guardrails on all open sides and fully planked platforms.
4.2 Scaffold inspection. Scaffolds are inspected by a competent person before each shift.

Section 5. Excavations and trenches
5.1 Protective systems. Trenches five feet deep or more are protected by sloping, shoring or a trench box.
5.2 Trench exit. A ladder or other safe means of exit is within 25 feet of every worker in a trench four feet deep or more.
5.3 Edge clearance. Spoil piles and equipment are kept at least two feet back from the trench edge.

Section 6. Rigging and lifting
6.1 Rigging inspection. Slings, shackles, eyebolts and hooks are inspected before each use.
6.2 Damaged slings. Web slings with cuts, frayed edges, burns or exposed red core yarns are removed from service.
6.3 Eyebolts. Eyebolts are rated, forged, shouldered eyebolts loaded in line with the shank; bent, stretched, or improvised eyebolts are removed from service.
6.4 Chain links. Chain links and end links that are stretched, warped or cracked are removed from service.

Section 7. Housekeeping and site
7.1 Clear walkways. Walkways and work areas are kept clear of debris, cords and loose material.
7.2 Secured materials. Materials stored on elevated surfaces are secured against falling.
7.3 Safety briefing. Every crew holds a safety briefing before starting high-risk work.
